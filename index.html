The engagement

Fast support still begins with careful verification.

I supported a European cosmetics ecommerce business through a short-term outsourced contract arrangement completed outside Upwork. The catalog included mascara and other cosmetics products, while the daily work centered on the customer experience after an order was placed.

I used Zendesk to manage customer emails, Shopify to investigate order and fulfillment details, and Google Sheets to log ticket updates after replies. DeepL and ChatGPT assisted with localized communication and drafting, while the final response and operational decision remained my responsibility.

The work followed an eight-hour split-shift pattern from Monday through Saturday, with at least an hour of Sunday coverage for follow-ups. The operating target was more than 15 tickets per hour, requiring concise investigation, accurate documentation, and consistent use of the support SOP. The client timezone was not formally recorded, but every market supported was aligned to Central European Time (UTC+1) during the engagement. My 11:00 AM–3:00 PM and 7:00–11:00 PM Philippine shifts therefore aligned approximately to 4:00–8:00 AM and 12:00–4:00 PM CET.

Why this work is not visible in Upwork history The engagement was completed through an off-platform outsourcing arrangement. I am now building my own independent client relationships, so I present this experience as a privacy-protected case study rather than an Upwork contract record.

Daily operating workflow

From inbox question to documented resolution.

A good response depended on connecting the customer’s message to the actual order state, payment method, fulfillment history, and the correct SOP path.

03 · CLASSIFY

Update status and log

Set the appropriate Zendesk state, record the action in Sheets, and preserve the next follow-up requirement.

Follow-up discipline

Pending tickets were reviewed every day—not left to memory.

  1. Initial reply

    Request or provide what is needed

    Send the first complete response and leave the ticket in the appropriate waiting state.

  2. Follow-up 1

    Recheck the next day

    Review the queue and send the first follow-up when the customer has not responded.

  3. Follow-up 2

    Send the third message

    Complete the second follow-up according to the SOP and preserve the final review date.

  4. Next review

    Mark as SOLVED with context

    If no response arrives, close the active work by marking the ticket SOLVED with a customer-unresponsive note.

Payment-method awareness

A refund request was not one universal process.

The correct path depended on how the customer paid. The support workflow used separate return and refund paths for Klarna, PayPal, and card or Shopify payments. Klarna orders required different and often faster coordination because the customer’s invoice, payment schedule, or dispute could remain active while the merchant-side order issue was being resolved.

Before communicating a resolution, I checked the Shopify order, confirmed the payment context, checked whether a dispute was open, followed the relevant SOP, and documented the action. This prevented a familiar support mistake: promising a standard refund timeline without accounting for the payment provider involved.

Anonymized case snapshots

Examples of how the work was approached.

These are reconstructed scenarios representing recurring work patterns. They contain no original customer identity, order number, or confidential company data.

DELIVERY

“Where is my order?”

Investigation: Match the order in Shopify, check fulfillment date and carrier tracking, then identify whether the parcel is moving, delayed, or requires escalation.

Response: Give the customer the verified status and next step without inventing a delivery date.

REFUND

Klarna order requires cancellation or refund

Investigation: Confirm fulfillment and payment method, then apply the payment-specific SOP promptly.

Response: Explain what was actioned, what the customer may still see in Klarna, and when further follow-up is appropriate.

ORDER ISSUE

Damaged, missing, or incorrect cosmetic item

Investigation: Compare the customer’s report with the Shopify order and collect the evidence required by the replacement or refund policy.

Response: Confirm the approved next step, set expectations, and document the resolution or escalation.

DELIVERED?

Carrier marks the parcel delivered, but the customer cannot find it

Investigation: Verify tracking, ask the fulfillment contact to obtain proof of delivery from the 3PL, and guide the customer through local checks.

Response: Share only verified delivery evidence and apply the approved resolution path if the parcel remains missing.

PROMOTION

Customer expected two mascaras from a 1+1 promotion

Investigation: Check the Shopify cart and order lines to determine whether two units were added or only one.

Response: Explain the promotion clearly and apply the appropriate remedy based on the payment method and internal policy.

FOLLOW-UP

Customer stops responding

Investigation: Review prior messages and the daily follow-up log before contacting the customer again.

Response: Follow the two-reminder SOP, then mark SOLVED with a customer-unresponsive note on the next review if no reply arrives.

Support principles

What I carried into every ticket.

Verify before promising

Customer-friendly wording cannot compensate for an unverified order or payment state.

Use AI as assistance

DeepL and ChatGPT supported communication; I remained accountable for accuracy and the final send.

Match the company tone

Messages were personalized, warm, and reassuring for a customer base that was predominantly women shopping for mascara and related cosmetics. No age range is assumed.

Document the next action

Zendesk status and the Sheets log kept follow-up work visible across daily queue reviews.

Know when to escalate

Exceptions, unclear policy, and actions beyond agent authority were safer to escalate than guess.
